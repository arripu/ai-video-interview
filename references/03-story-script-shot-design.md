# Story, Script, And Shot Design

Use this when the user needs a story, script, visual sequence, storyboard, or keyframe plan.

## From Vague Idea To Structure

Extract or ask for:

- purpose
- protagonist
- object of desire
- conflict or tension
- emotional shift
- product/brand role
- memorable visual moment
- ending or call to action

If the user has only a rough idea, propose 2-3 structures before writing detailed prompts.

## Common Structures

- **Problem to solution**: pain point, product/character intervention, proof, result.
- **Reality to imagination**: real-world trigger, dream/fantasy transition, emotional journey, return/reveal.
- **Character arrival**: environment, tease, reveal, signature action, hero pose, brand close.
- **Product ritual**: setup, tactile use, benefit moment, lifestyle proof, pack/logo.
- **Before/after**: old state, transformation, new state, emotional confirmation.
- **Adventure route**: invitation, departure, challenge, collaboration, reward.
- **Mood film**: image sequence led by atmosphere rather than plot.

## Shot List Fields

Use these fields for a professional text storyboard:

| Shot | Time | Scene | Shot size | Camera | Movement | Action | Visual purpose | Assets | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep each shot tied to a purpose. If a shot does not serve story, product, rhythm, or emotion, cut or merge it.

## Keyframe Planning

For image-to-video workflows, define:

- start and end frame intent
- action continuity
- subject position
- camera direction
- scale changes
- lighting/time continuity
- source assets for each frame
- frames that require user approval before expanding

Avoid writing prompts for 20+ frames before the first spatial and character rules are locked.

## Supported Deliverable Shapes

- **Single calibration frame**: one image to test asset, style, and tone.
- **4 style explorations**: A/B/C/D visual directions with short recommendation.
- **12-panel board**: one 4x3 overview for a 15s or 30s segment.
- **24 keyframes**: independent 16:9 or 9:16 frames for a detailed video segment.
- **15-second nodes**: video tool prompts grouped by time segments.
- **Client plan**: clean concept, structure, visual direction, and execution notes without full prompt clutter.

## Common Failure Prevention

- Do not introduce characters into empty facility or product asset shots unless the user asks.
- Do not move fixed props or characters across a room without a transition.
- Do not change a vehicle, prop, or product into a different object for convenience.
- Do not let a montage erase the user's required story logic.
- Do not treat "make it more beautiful" as permission to remove required elements.
