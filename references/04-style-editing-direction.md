# Style And Editing Direction

Use this before visual prompts, especially when the user mentions reference works, desired mood, or editing style.

## Decode References

When the user gives a film, ad, image, or video reference, identify which dimension matters:

- color palette
- lighting
- lens and camera movement
- composition
- texture/material
- character acting
- editing rhythm
- sound/music mood
- typography
- worldbuilding
- emotional tone

Ask if unclear. A reference rarely means copying all dimensions.

## Editing Questions

- Should the rhythm feel like a short ad, film trailer, children's animation, social hook, slow emotional film, product demo, or music-driven montage?
- Is the first 3 seconds a hook, atmosphere setup, product reveal, or story setup?
- Should transitions be invisible, magical, match-cut, whip-pan, light-flash, object-driven, title-card, or hard cut?
- Does the video need a final packshot, logo lockup, tagline, subtitle card, or call to action?
- Should music lead the cuts, or should action/story lead the music?

## Audio And Text

Clarify whether the project needs:

- voiceover
- dialogue
- subtitles
- on-screen labels
- title cards
- end card
- BGM prompt
- sound effects list
- lyrics or no lyrics
- Suno or other music prompt

For client-facing drafts, keep copy concise and strategic. For internal production docs, preserve full prompt and timing detail.

## Style Danger Zones

Always write project-specific negative rules when relevant:

- AI noise and repeated artifacts
- identity drift
- cheap 3D or plastic render
- unfused pasted materials
- unreadable or invented text
- horror pressure when the tone should be warm
- over-crowded frame
- random new props
- inconsistent time of day
- independent-poster look across video frames

## Exploration Strategy

Use one of these before batch work:

- **1 calibration frame** when the direction is mostly known.
- **4 style options** when tone, palette, or lighting is uncertain.
- **reference board first** when asset fidelity matters more than style.
- **text-only storyboard first** when story logic is still unstable.
